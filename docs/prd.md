# Deliberative Process Design Tool (DPDT)

The DPDT is a web-based decision support system designed to help practitioners and public authorities design large-scale deliberative processes (e.g., mini-publics, deliberative polls, citizen juries) by translating deliberative principles into concrete process recommendations. DPDT applies multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) to score and rank deliberative design templates and recommend appropriate process configurations based on practical constraints and priorities across five deliberative principles.

## Theoretical Background

### Deliberation Principles

These principles must be supported to achieve a high-quality deliberative process.

- _equality_: participants must have equal opportunity to participate
- _inclusion_: participants must be representative of the entire population, including marginalised perspectives
- _plurality_: the arguments, experiences, and discourses must be representative of the entire population
- _authenticity_: participants must be sincere, respect each other, and use non-coercive communication
- _reflection_: participants must weigh arguments, reconsider each others' preferences, and integrate competing reasons into a collective judgments

### Deliberation Process Stages

Deliberation happens in stages: selection, pre-deliberation, deliberation, and post-deliberation. Across these stages, there are 9 processes that must be supported to achieve a high-quality deliberative process:

1. _selection_:

   1.1. _recruitment_: participants must be invited to participate

2. _pre-deliberation_

   2.1. _orientation_: participants must be introduced to the process, including its objectives, rules, and expectations

   2.2. _group-building_: participants must get to know each other so that they can achieve the necessary levels of trust, cohesion, and communicative readiness to deliberate

   2.3. _information & learning_: participants must exchange of relevant knowledge and evidence

3. _deliberation_

   3.1. _problem definition_: participants must collectively frame and clarify of the issue under discussion

   3.2. _discussion_: participants must exchange and evaluate arguments and perspectives

   3.3. _solution_: participants must collectively develop and refine proposals or recommendations

4. _post-deliberation_

   4.1. _output generation_: participants must synthesize and formalize collective outputs

   4.2. _review & communication_: deliberation organizers must gather feedback from participants, evaluate the process, and disseminate the process outcomes to the entire population

### Tensions

As deliberation size increases, there are four core tensions that deliberation designers must account for:

- _false equality and inclusion_: when deliberation scales, especially online, those who have digital skills have an advantage in participation; online gives a false sense of "anyone can participate"

- _lower sense of community_: authenticity may decrease in larger communities

- _decreased reflection_: expanding participation increases plurality and representativeness, but often reduces sustained interaction and reflective exchange.

- _small perceived contribution_: participants might not be motivated to participate if they perceive the size of their contribution to be small.
